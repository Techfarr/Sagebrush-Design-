The design problem

Teach people to investigate evidence before they settle on a story.

The experience was built around a simple behavior: separate what the evidence supports from what is still assumption, then keep investigating. Rather than showing every screen from a large curriculum, this sample focuses on the visual evidence that best demonstrates that design.

Digital learning

Fact or assumption practice.

Learners sort short statements according to what the evidence actually supports. The public reconstruction keeps the same Rise interaction structure and information hierarchy.

Changed for public use: client branding, palette, names, and proprietary examples.

Facilitated learning

The Reframe.

New evidence arrives only after learners have built an initial finding. The group has to test whether that finding still holds and consider where the contributing factors actually sit.

Preserved from the delivered ILT: the delayed reveal, SHIELD rail, and three causation levels. The incident details are fictionalized.

Performance support

Quick Reference Guide.

The learning was designed to remain useful after class. The QRG gives supervisors a compact investigation sequence and the core questions they need when an incident occurs.

Changed for public use: internal contacts, deadlines, system names, policy language, and client branding. The document structure is retained.

Learner practice materials

The worksheets carried the investigation, not just the notes.

These were thinking guides used while learners worked the case. They were deliberately form-agnostic rather than copies of a reporting system, so the practice stayed focused on evidence, gaps, and report quality.

Physical Evidence

Observation, evidence classification, and unanswered questions are captured in the same worksheet.

Digital Evidence

Learners log what happens on screen, classify what it supports, and explicitly record what the camera cannot show.

Report Writing

The report is built only from what the group documented and verified, using the 5 W's as the narrative frame.

SharePoint resource environment

Evidence during class. Reference after class.

The post-pilot redesign moved evidence out of QR/passcode gates and into a view-only SharePoint environment. Evidence pages followed the same category language used in the eLearning, while downloadable job aids were kept in a separate reference area.

Public-safe reconstruction: this preserves the documented information architecture and visual treatment without reproducing the client site, internal URLs, real evidence, or proprietary procedures. The exact client page set is intentionally not reproduced.

How the pieces worked together

The learning continued beyond the screen.

Each format had a distinct job. The eLearning established concepts. The facilitated session made people apply them. The resource environment and job support stayed available afterward.
