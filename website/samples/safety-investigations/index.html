The design problem

Teach people to investigate evidence before they settle on a story.

The experience was built around a simple behavior: separate what the evidence supports from what is still assumption, then keep investigating. Rather than showing every screen from a large curriculum, this sample focuses on the visual evidence that best demonstrates that design.

Digital learning

Evidence before conclusions.

The learner is asked to distinguish evidence from assumption and challenge an early interpretation before moving forward.

Faithfulness rule: layout and interaction intent stay aligned to the original deliverable; only identifying content and palette are changed.

Framework + performance support

One process across every format.

SHIELD gave learners a simple sequence they could use in the eLearning, during facilitated practice, and later as a quick reference during real work.

Secure the Scene · Health Check · Inform · Evidence · Listen · Document

Pilot + validation

Change the delivery when the pilot says to.

The pilot showed that an open escape-room-style experience created too much uncertainty for this audience. The redesign kept discovery and evidence gathering but added facilitator pacing and calibration.

The case study reports learner reaction and intent to apply. It does not claim operational improvement that was not measured.

Learning ecosystem

A system, not a single course.

The final design connected short digital lessons with a four-hour application experience, facilitator guidance, learner worksheets, a resource environment, and quick-reference support.

The public sample uses real project architecture and sanitized captures rather than invented product UI.

How the pieces worked together

The learning continued beyond the screen.

Each format had a distinct job. The eLearning established concepts. The facilitated session made people apply them. The resource environment and job support stayed available afterward.
