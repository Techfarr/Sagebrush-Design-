## 1. Purpose

The website has one primary business goal:

**Convert prospective clients into qualified conversations for contract learning strategy, instructional design, project recovery, and related consulting work.**

The site should make a prospective client quickly understand:

- What problems Sagebrush Design solves.
- What Samantha personally does.
- What kinds of organizations and operating environments she has worked in.
- What the work actually looked like.
- What decisions and constraints shaped the solution.
- What evidence exists that the work was useful or successful.
- What is not being claimed when outcomes were not measured.
- How to contact Samantha.

The portfolio should look like evidence of real work, not a collection of invented marketing mockups.

## 2. Non-Negotiable Rebuild Rules

These rules are locked unless Samantha explicitly reopens them.

### 2.1 Change control
1. Work only on the current approved task.
2. Do not remove, replace, redesign, or materially alter an approved section unless the current task requires it.
3. Do not silently change page architecture, visual direction, project hierarchy, or positioning.
4. New ideas go into the Parking Lot. They are not implemented during the current task.
5. Before editing, state:
   - Current task ID.
   - What is in scope.
   - What is not in scope.
6. After editing, report:
   - What changed.
   - What remains.
   - What the next task is.
7. If a requested edit conflicts with a locked decision, flag the conflict before changing it.

### 2.2 Portfolio visual fidelity
For real client work, the actual deliverable is the visual source of truth.

Allowed:
- Remove logos and client/company names.
- Replace proprietary names, data, screenshots, URLs, procedures, or identifying information.
- Change the client-branded color palette.
- Replace copyrighted/proprietary imagery when necessary.
- Recreate a screen when the original cannot be publicly shown.

Not allowed:
- Redesign the deliverable into a different-looking course or interaction.
- Invent a prettier AI-style interface.
- Change the interaction type, layout logic, information hierarchy, or overall structure just to make it look more polished.
- Present a reconstruction as an original screenshot.

Public reconstructions must be labeled honestly when needed.

### 2.3 Evidence and claims
- Use verified metrics only.
- Separate learner reaction, stakeholder feedback, delivery evidence, and business outcomes.
- Do not imply operational results that were not measured.
- Do not publish confidential client names, logos, internal systems, employee names, URLs, or proprietary content.
- The master portfolio inventory is the factual source of truth for public claims.

### 2.4 Copy rules
Copy should sound like an experienced consultant describing work she actually performed.

Prefer:
- Concrete project language.
- Specific constraints.
- Specific decisions.
- Specific deliverables.
- Clear role ownership.
- Simple explanations of why the design worked the way it did.

Avoid:
- Generic consulting language.
- Repeated "not X, but Y" constructions.
- Repeated three-part slogan structures.
- Overuse of punchy fragments.
- Marketing claims unsupported by evidence.
- Copy that sounds artificially polished or interchangeable with another consultant's site.
- Em dashes in public-facing copy when a period, comma, or colon works instead.

## 3. Locked Brand and Positioning Decisions

### 3.1 Business positioning
**Principal-led, one-person expert consultancy with strategy and build capability.**

Samantha may lead, design, develop, troubleshoot, recover, and manage work directly. Enterprise-scale leadership experience should be shown without implying Sagebrush Design was the prime contractor when Samantha worked as a subcontractor.

### 3.2 Core positioning
Sector-neutral homepage.

Primary capabilities:
- Learning strategy.
- Instructional design and development.
- Complex project recovery.
- AI-supported development where appropriate.
- Learning systems / performance support when the problem requires more than a course.

### 3.3 Visual identity
Locked:
- High-desert visual direction.
- Sora for headings.
- Source Sans 3 for body copy.
- Desert hero direction.
- No gradients.
- Professional, grounded, operational feel.
- Agriculture credibility without making the entire site look like an agriculture-only business.

### 3.4 Homepage rules
Locked:
- Safety Investigations is the homepage flagship.
- Featured Work must remain on the homepage.
- Homepage remains sector-neutral.
- Agriculture remains a dedicated homepage section.
- Ag Dirt Busters remains linked as a featured agriculture project.
- Do not make 508/accessibility a homepage selling point.
- About section should reinforce principal-led consulting.
- Working-context portrait required before launch.
- Contact CTA should lead to a simple problem-based inquiry, not a product menu.

### 3.5 Public portfolio scope
Primary / featured case studies:
1. Safety Investigations.
2. Large-Scale Learning Project Recovery.
3. Contract Fundamentals.
4. Ag Dirt Busters.

Supporting proof:
- VA / government scenario and systems learning.
- NERC CIP learning library strategy.
- Rural healthcare policy system.

Do not restore as primary public cases unless Samantha explicitly changes the plan:
- Virtual School Launch.
- Compliance / Oil-Field training.

## 8. Approval Gates

Nothing moves to DONE until the relevant gate is passed.

### Visual gate
- Does it resemble the actual deliverable?
- Are proprietary elements removed?
- Are colors changed only as needed?
- Does it avoid an AI-generated/mockup look?
- Is the image meaningfully different from nearby images?

### Content gate
- Is Samantha's role clear?
- Is the business/performance problem clear?
- Are the decisions specific?
- Are claims supported?
- Does the copy sound natural and project-specific?

### Client gate
- Would a prospective client understand why this work matters?
- Is the page easy to scan?
- Does the evidence create trust?
- Is the next action obvious?
- Is anything included only because it looks impressive rather than because it helps the client decide?
